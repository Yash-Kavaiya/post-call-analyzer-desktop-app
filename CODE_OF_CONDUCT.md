# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they are trying to help. Say what you mean about
the work, not about the person who did it.

## What is expected

- **Critique the code, not the author.** "This drops the last segment when the
  buffer is empty" is a review. "This is sloppy" is not.
- **Assume good faith.** Most disagreements in a code review are two people
  holding different context, not one person being careless.
- **Help newcomers.** Everyone's first pull request to a Rust workspace with
  fourteen crates is confusing. Point at the file, not at the documentation.
- **Accept a decision once it is made.** Argue your case fully, then move on
  if it goes the other way.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, pull requests, commit
  messages, or anywhere else this project's name is attached.
- Discrimination or derogatory comments on the basis of anything a person did
  not choose about themselves.
- Publishing anyone's private information.
- Posting real customer data — call recordings, transcripts, or anything
  containing personal information — in a public issue or pull request. This
  project exists to process that data carefully; leaking it here would be a
  particular kind of failure.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments, or making the tracker
  unusable for everyone else.

## Reporting

Report anything that concerns you to **yash.kavaiya3@gmail.com**.

Reports are read by the maintainer and kept confidential. You will get a reply.
If a report concerns the maintainer, say so and it will be handled with an
uninvolved third party.

## Consequences

The maintainer may edit or remove comments, commits, code, and issues that
break these rules, and may temporarily or permanently block anyone whose
behaviour warrants it. Where the reason is not obvious, it will be explained.
